# Medical SAM3 (arXiv:2601.10880) and cross-modality / prompt-type transfer evaluation for promptable medical segmentation

Method note: arxiv.org, huggingface.co and most web hosts could not be reached with WebFetch in this session. Primary evidence came from (1) the arXiv PDF and metadata, fetched with curl from export.arxiv.org and read in full, (2) a shallow `git clone` of the official GitHub repo (last commit 2026-09-08, "Add standalone 3D inference pipeline"), and (3) PDFs of the closest related papers, also from export.arxiv.org. ## Q1. What Medical SAM3 is and what it covers

### Takeaway
The paper exists and the card cites it correctly. Medical SAM3 fully fine-tunes Meta's SAM 3 on 33 public 2D datasets across 10 modalities, with text-only prompts. The decisive finding for the card is that ultrasound and dermoscopy are not untested in this paper. Both are in the training corpus (BUSI, BUS-UCLM, ISIC 2018 and others) and both are in the external zero-shot test set (TN3K and HC18 for ultrasound, PH2 for dermoscopy). Kvasir-SEG, the student team's dataset, is also in the training corpus. What the paper does not do is compare point, box and text prompts for its own model. Every reported number uses text only.

### Cited Findings
**Identity and status**
- Exact title: "Medical SAM3: A Foundation Model for Universal Prompt-Driven Medical Image Segmentation". arXiv:2601.10880 [cs.CV]. There is only one version (v1), submitted Thu 15 Jan 2026 22:18 UTC by Yu Tian. The arXiv page lists no venue or journal ref, so it is a preprint, and no acceptance was found. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10880)
- Authors: Chongcong Jiang*, Tianxingjian Ding*, Chuhan Song*, Jiachen Tu, Ziyang Yan†, Yihua Shao, Zhenyi Wang, Yuzhang Shang, Tianyu Han, Yu Tian†. Affiliations: UCF, UCL, UIUC, Trento, PolyU and UPenn. Corresponding authors are Z. Yan and Y. Tian (yu.tian2@ucf.edu). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10880)
- **Name collision to flag:** a different, earlier paper is titled "MedSAM3: Delving into Segment Anything with Medical Concepts" (arXiv:2511.19046). It was submitted 2025-11-24 and updated 2026-09-14, by Anglin Liu, Xu R. Cao, Yifan Shen, Yi Lu, Xiang Li, Qianqian Chen and Jintai Chen, with code at github.com/Joey-S-Liu/MedSAM3. The two are separate works, and students and search engines often confuse them. — [arXiv 2511.19046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19046)

**Base model and method**
- Base model: SAM 3 ("SAM 3: Segment Anything with Concepts", Carion et al., arXiv:2511.16719), with all parameters fine-tuned and "no architectural modifications". It keeps SAM 3's detector plus tracker/memory-bank design for slice-based 3D. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10880)
- Training setup: 1008×1008 resolution, and layer-wise LR decay of γ=0.85 over L=12 layers. AdamW with these learning rates: 3e-4 for the decoder, segmentation head and scoring; 5e-5 for the vision backbone; 5e-5 for the language backbone; 1e-4 for the geometry prompt encoder. Up to 10 epochs on one node with 4× NVIDIA H100 80 GB. The loss is a set-prediction objective (Hungarian plus an auxiliary one-to-many matcher, focal, Dice and presence terms). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10880)
- Prompts in the paper: "For both training and evaluation, we use text-only prompts for all internal and external tasks," with "a single canonical concept term per class". "Training uses only text prompts... without any spatial or interactive prompts such as points or bounding boxes." At evaluation, the highest-confidence mask is kept. Multi-class images are queried once per class, and overlaps go to the class with the highest pixel-wise confidence. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10880)
- Prompt support in the architecture and code: SAM 3 accepts points, boxes and text. Medical SAM3's 2D evaluation script runs both GT-derived box prompts and text prompts ("Evaluates MedSAM3 with custom checkpoints using both box prompts and text prompts") on CHASE_DB1, STARE, CVC-ClinicDB, ETIS-Larib and PH2. The box comes from the GT mask through `generate_bbox_from_mask`. The released 2D eval code has no point-prompt path. — [repo inference/run_medsam3_evaluation.py](https://github.com/AIM-Research-Lab/Medical-SAM3)
- Possible inconsistency: the README for the later 3D "V2" training config says "`TextQueryToVisual` probability 0.5 — text + noisy GT box on half the steps". The paper says training was text-only. The two statements may describe different model versions (2D v1 versus the 3D v2 released in 2026-06), but this is unresolved. — [repo medical/training/README.md](https://github.com/AIM-Research-Lab/Medical-SAM3)

**Training data (Table 1 in the PDF): 33 datasets, 76,956 images, 263,705 masks, fixed 85/15 train/val split with seed 42 (about 65.4k training and 11.5k validation images)**
- CXR: COVID-QU-Ex (33,920 images), Chest Xray Masks and Labels, Chest X-Ray Pneumothorax. X-ray: BTXRD, ARCADE.
- **Ultrasound (7): BUSI (647), BUS-UCLM (264), US-Nerve (2,323), ACOUSLIC (300), PS-FH-AOP-2023 (4,000), CT2USforKidneySeg (4,586), SegThy (59)**, for a total of 12,179 ultrasound images.
- **Endoscopy (6 per the header; 5 listed): CholecSeg8k, m2caiSeg, PolypGen, BKAI-IGH NeoPolyp, Kvasir-SEG (1,000 images / 1,060 masks).**
- Pathology: PanNuke, MoNuSeg, Breast Cancer Segmentation, GlaS. Virtual microscopy: MUCIC Colon and HL60. EM: NucMM-Z, UroCell. Microscopy: PCMMD. Fundus/OCT: Intraretinal Cystoid Fluid, PAPILA, COph100, RAVIR, DRIVE.
- **Dermoscopy and others: ISIC 2018 (2,594 images), FetoPlac.**
- Source for all four items above: [arXiv PDF, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10880). The repo training YAML confirms paths for BUSI, ISIC_2018, Kvasir-SEG, PolypGen and COVID-QU-Ex: [repo sam3/train/configs/medsam3_stage1_train_all_unified.yaml](https://github.com/AIM-Research-Lab/Medical-SAM3)
- The section headers in Table 1 sum to 34 datasets against the 33 claimed, and the endoscopy header says 6 but lists 5. These are minor internal inconsistencies in the PDF.

**Benchmark and reported results (Table 3 in the PDF; text-only prompts; Dice %, SAM3 → Medical SAM3)**
- Internal held-out tasks (10): PS-FH-AOP'23 (ultrasound) 65.7→91.6; DRIVE 24.8→55.8; COph100 34.1→63.1; Breast Cancer (pathology) 16.3→43.8; Intraretinal Fluid 62.0→85.0; M2CAI 67.7→88.1; FetoPlac 56.6→77.0; GlaS 68.9→88.2; SegThy (ultrasound) 57.3→78.5; PAPILA 86.2→99.4. Average Dice 54.0→77.0 and average IoU 43.3→67.3. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10880)
- External tasks (7), excluded from training: **TN3K (thyroid US) 4.2→40.8; HC18 (fetal-head US) 23.9→92.6**; CVC-ClinicDB 0.0→87.9; ETIS-Larib 0.0→86.1; **PH2 (dermoscopy) 18.4→92.7**; CHASE_DB1 17.9→62.6; STARE 18.6→54.4. Average Dice 11.9→73.9 and average IoU 8.0→64.4. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10880)
- The paper's comparison set has no other medical baseline. It compares only vanilla SAM3 against Medical SAM3, with no MedSAM, BiomedParse, SAT, nnU-Net or similar. The internal benchmark is a held-out split of the training datasets, so it is in-distribution. The external sets are polyp sets whose sister datasets (Kvasir-SEG, PolypGen, BKAI) and dermoscopy sets whose sister dataset (ISIC 2018) are in training, so "external" means dataset-level shift, not modality-level shift. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10880)

**Failure modes and limitations the authors report**
- Vanilla SAM3's apparent competitiveness "largely rel[ies] on strong geometric priors such as ground-truth-derived bounding boxes". The authors list a "diagnostic study that characterizes the failure modes of vanilla SAM3" as a contribution. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10880)
- Qualitative failure modes of vanilla SAM3 under text prompts: near-empty masks; severe over-segmentation that collapses to large foreground regions; noisy, false-positive-heavy masks on thin vessels; missed or fragmented small endoscopic targets; spurious background activation on dermoscopy. Failures that remain after fine-tuning are concentrated on "small, thin, or low-contrast targets". — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10880)
- Stated limitations: high compute cost for full adaptation at high resolution; the planar (2D) formulation underuses volumetric continuity; evaluation covers only atomic concept prompts, with synonym-robust, attribute-rich and compositional prompts left as future work; multi-center validation and uncertainty estimation are still needed. The authors also call for benchmarks that separate interactive (spatial-hint) settings from semantic-only settings, and for "standardized prompt protocols". — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10880)

There is a project page at chongcongjiang.github.io/MedicalSAM3 and an HF link at huggingface.co/Chongcong/Medical-SAM3. — [repo README](https://github.com/AIM-Research-Lab/Medical-SAM3)

### Inferences
- The card's premise that ultrasound and dermoscopy are "untested public modalit[ies]" is false for Medical SAM3. Both modalities appear in training and in external evaluation. Under the paper's own numbers, the external dermoscopy (PH2, 92.7 Dice) and fetal-head ultrasound (HC18, 92.6) results are already strong. Thyroid ultrasound (TN3K, 40.8 Dice) is a clear weak point and the most promising direction for a modality-level extension.
- The open gap that remains in Medical SAM3 itself is the prompt type. The paper reports text-only results. The code can run GT-box prompts on five 2D external sets, but box numbers for the fine-tuned model are not reported in the paper, and points are absent entirely. A point/box/text comparison for Medical SAM3 is therefore still unpublished by the authors, but related work below covers this for vanilla SAM3, so the novelty is thin.
- Kvasir-SEG is in Medical SAM3's training data. A student project fine-tuning SAM ViT-B on Kvasir-SEG is in-distribution for this paper, not a transfer test, and it uses SAM (v1), not SAM 3.

## Q2. Is the public benchmark reproducible?

### Takeaway
Partly. The 2D evaluation (the 7 external sets plus a few extra loaders) has runnable scripts, fixed text prompts and a bundled SAM3. But the internal 85/15 split files for the 33-dataset 2D training corpus are not distributed as standalone files; they exist only as seed-42 split logic and absolute cluster paths in a YAML. The training code released in the repo targets the later 3D "V2" model on CVPR BiomedSegFM data. Evaluating the external sets with a released checkpoint is feasible on one GPU. Retraining the paper model requires 4×H100 80 GB and re-collecting 33 datasets.

### Cited Findings
- 2D eval toolkit: `inference/run_medsam3_evaluation.py --checkpoint ... --model-name medsam3`, with options `--max-samples` and `--datasets`. The data path is `../medsam_data/`, and the README does not explain how to download or arrange the data. — [repo inference/README.md](https://github.com/AIM-Research-Lab/Medical-SAM3)
- Fixed prompt dictionary: CHASE_DB1/STARE "Retinal Blood Vessel"; CVC-ClinicDB/ETIS-Larib "Polyp"; PH2 "Skin Lesion"; TN3K/DDTI/TG3K "thyroid nodule"; HC18 "fetal head"; DSB18/CoNSeP "cell nucleus". Loaders exist for DDTI, TG3K, DSB18 and CoNSeP, which the paper does not report. — [repo inference/dataset_loaders.py](https://github.com/AIM-Research-Lab/Medical-SAM3)
- Training: `python sam3/train/train.py -c configs/medsam3_stage1_train_all_unified --num-gpus 4`. The YAML "contains absolute paths from the development cluster" (for example /anvil/projects/...) that must be patched with a script. Training requires "CVPR BiomedSegFM converted data" plus per-dataset JSON annotations, and an "Optional init weights... sam3_stage0.pt". — [repo medical/training/README.md, experiments/README.md](https://github.com/AIM-Research-Lab/Medical-SAM3)
- Compute reported in the paper: 1 node × 4 H100 80 GB, up to 10 epochs. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10880)

### Inferences
- The most reproducible "Reproduce" target is the 7-dataset external text-prompt table (Table 3, bottom), using the released checkpoint and the repo's 2D evaluation. Getting TN3K, HC18, PH2, CVC-ClinicDB, ETIS-Larib, CHASE_DB1 and STARE is the main manual effort. Reproducing the internal table requires rebuilding the seed-42 85/15 splits from 33 datasets, some of which (for example COVID-QU-Ex at 33.9k images) are large.
- SAM 3 inference at 1008×1008 is heavier than SAM ViT-B, and a 24 GB-class GPU is probably needed for comfortable inference. This is an estimate and was not measured.

### Gaps
- Checkpoint size, inference VRAM and runtime per image are not reported.
- It is unverified whether the HF checkpoint is the exact one behind Table 3, rather than the later V2.

## Q3. What prior and concurrent work already evaluates promptable medical segmentation across modalities and prompt types?

### Takeaway
Cross-modality evaluation that includes ultrasound and dermoscopy, and comparisons of point versus box prompts, are heavily covered for SAM and SAM 2. Studies from 2023–2024 already report "box > point" and correlate failure with boundary complexity and contrast. For SAM 3, at least five 2025–2026 papers compare text against visual prompts, several of them on BUSI and ISIC 2018 specifically. UltraSAM3 (July 2026) already benchmarks Medical SAM3 on external ultrasound sets. The space is crowded.

### Cited Findings
**SAM / SAM 2 era (point vs box, many modalities including US and dermoscopy)**
- Mazurowski et al., "Segment Anything Model for Medical Image Analysis: an Experimental Study" (arXiv:2304.10517; published in MedIA). Covers 19 datasets and reports that "SAM performs notably better with box prompts than with point prompts". Performance is better for "well-circumscribed objects with prompts with less ambiguity". It also studies iterative multi-point prompting. — [arXiv 2304.10517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10517)
- Huang et al., "Segment Anything Model for Medical Images?" (MedIA; arXiv:2304.14660). The COSMOS 1050K benchmark has 53 datasets, 18 modalities and 84 objects. Findings: box beats Everything mode; sensitivity to center-point randomness and to tight boxes; performance "correlated to different factors, including boundary complexity, intensity differences". This is effectively a failure-factor analysis. — [arXiv 2304.14660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14660)
- Cheng et al., "SAM on Medical Images: A Comprehensive Study on Three Prompt Modes" (arXiv:2305.00035). Covers 12+ datasets and explores "what kind of prompt can lead to the best zero-shot performance with different modalities", which is a per-modality prompt-selection question. It reports that box-size perturbation changes accuracy significantly. — [arXiv 2305.00035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00035)
- Ma et al., "Segment Anything in Medical Images and Videos: Benchmark and Deployment" (arXiv:2408.03322). Benchmarks SAM2 across 11 modalities against SAM1 and MedSAM. A search snippet says dermoscopy results are high and that video failures arise from first-frame failure, unclear boundaries and low contrast. — [arXiv 2408.03322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03322); snippet via [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/382914820_Segment_Anything_in_Medical_Images_and_Videos_Benchmark_and_Deployment)
- Quesada et al., "Benchmarking Human and Automated Prompting in SAM" (arXiv:2410.22048). Human prompts score about 29% higher than automated point-selection strategies, and the paper identifies prompt features predictive of performance (R² > 0.5). It concerns automated point-prompt selection in general, not medical. — [arXiv 2410.22048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22048)

**Medical foundation models (cross-modality, by prompt type)**
- MedSAM (arXiv:2304.12306): 1.57M image-mask pairs, 10 modalities, 86 internal and 60 external validation tasks. Box-prompted. — [arXiv 2304.12306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12306)
- SAM-Med2D (arXiv:2308.16184): about 4.6M images and 19.7M masks; fine-tuned with box, point and mask prompts; evaluated across modalities and on 9 MICCAI 2023 challenge datasets. — [arXiv 2308.16184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.16184)
- SAM-Med3D (arXiv:2310.15161): 3D point prompts; 16 evaluation datasets. — [arXiv 2310.15161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15161)
- MedSAM2 (arXiv:2504.03600): 3D and video, including a user study on 251,550 echocardiogram frames, which is ultrasound video. — [arXiv 2504.03600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03600)
- BiomedParse (Nature Methods 2024; arXiv:2405.12971): text-prompted segmentation and detection of 82 object types across 9 modalities, with 102,855 test triples. — [arXiv 2405.12971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12971)
- SAT, "Large-Vocabulary Segmentation for Medical Images with Text Prompts" (arXiv:2312.17183): 3D, text-prompted, 72 datasets and 497 classes; compared against box-prompted MedSAM. — [arXiv 2312.17183](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17183)

**SAM 3 era: text vs visual prompts on medical data (closest to the card)**
- MedSAM3 by Liu et al. (arXiv:2511.19046), not to be confused with the base paper. Vanilla SAM 3 with text only (T) versus text plus GT box (T+I), on 11 2D datasets. BUSI: T 0 Dice against T+I 0.7110. ISIC 2018: T 0.2189 against T+I 0.8178. Kvasir-SEG: T 0 against T+I 0.7671. The fine-tuned MedSAM-3 scores: BUSI T 0.2674 / T+I 0.7772; ISIC T 0.5687 / T+I 0.9058; Kvasir T 0.1441 / T+I 0.8831. The paper also gives named failure findings, including "Systematic Misalignment Between Concept Prompts and Anatomical Target Regions" (for example, the concept "lesion" on ISIC covers broad non-lesion areas and excludes the lesion) and "Limited Semantic Discrimination of Fine-Grained Medical Terminology". — [arXiv 2511.19046 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.19046)
  - A search summary stated "0.0803 on BUSI". In the PDF, 0.0803 belongs to the DSB 2018 column and BUSI is 0. This should be treated as a misattribution in the search snippet.
- Xu et al., "Adapting SAM3 for Concept-Driven Lesion Segmentation in Medical Images: An Experimental Study" (arXiv:2603.25945; code at apple1986/lesion-sam3). Covers 13 datasets and 11 lesion types across MRI, CT, **ultrasound (BUSI), dermoscopy (ISIC2018)** and endoscopy (Kvasir-SEG). It compares point, GT-box, text, image-exemplar and text+exemplar prompts, as well as fine-tuning strategies (partial, adapter, full). GT-box Dice for SAM2 against SAM3: ISIC2018 85.43 vs 90.82; BUSI 87.49 vs 87.41; Kvasir-SEG 94.33 vs 94.52. Text-only prompts "consistently yield the poorest segmentation performance", for example 0.69% Dice on BraTS2023-MET. — [arXiv 2603.25945 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.25945). A search snippet adds a wording-sensitivity example: the prompt "lesion" gave 62.36% Dice while "tumor" gave 0.00%. That example could not be traced in the PDF text. — [Springer VCIBA version](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42492-026-00234-0)
- Kong et al., "Is SAM3 ready for pathology segmentation?" (arXiv:2604.18225; accepted to ICIP 2026). Proposes "a systematic evaluation protocol" covering zero-shot, few-shot and supervised settings with varying prompt strategies. Findings: text-only prompts poorly activate nuclear concepts; results are "highly sensitive to visual prompt types and budgets"; the model is not robust to visual-prompt noise. A search summary says it recommends prioritizing box over point and increasing box quality and budget. — [arXiv 2604.18225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18225)
- Chakrabarty & Soni, "Comparing SAM 2 and SAM 3 for Zero-Shot Segmentation of 3D Medical Data" (arXiv:2511.21926). Covers 16 datasets (CT, MRI, **ultrasound**, endoscopy) and 54 structures, under click, box and mask prompting. It "quantif[ies] three failure modes: prompt-frame over-segmentation, over-propagation after object disappearance, and temporal retention". — [arXiv 2511.21926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21926)
- Dong et al., "More than Segmentation: Benchmarking SAM 3 ... in Robotic Surgery" (arXiv:2512.07596). Point, box and language prompts on EndoVis 2017/2018. Language prompts are "currently suboptimal". — [arXiv 2512.07596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07596)
- Xu et al., "UltraSAM3: A Concept-Driven Foundation Model for Universal Ultrasound Image Segmentation" (arXiv:2607.29200). Trained on 37 public ultrasound datasets covering 13 anatomical categories, plus an instruction-guided agent. It compares directly against **Medical SAM3**, SAM3, BiomedParse and UniBiomed, on an internal corpus and on 4 external ultrasound sets, and includes "visual-prompt-enhanced settings". External Dice (Medical SAM3 → UltraSAM3): BrEast 0.7066→0.7461; CCAUI carotid 0.6144→0.9543; 105US liver tumor 0.2901→0.3642; KFGNet thyroid 0.1577→0.5082. — [arXiv 2607.29200 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.29200)
- Lv et al., "From SAM 1 to SAM 3: Benchmarking Zero-Shot Cross-Domain Medical Image Segmentation" (Expert Systems, 2026). Only the title and a snippet were seen, so the details are unverified. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/exsy.70383)
- Other SAM3-medical adaptations found in arXiv listings (titles only): SAM3-Adapter (2511.19425); Dual-Adaptive SAM3 (2607.02571, MICCAI 2026); Mask to Concept, test-time concept-embedding search (2606.26711, MICCAI 2026); concept-alignment test-time adaptation of SAM3 (2606.22963); Prompt Group-Aware Training for text-guided nuclei (2603.06384); few-shot concept prompt learning (2608.01663). — [arXiv listing query](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=all:%22SAM3%22+AND+all:medical)
- A conversational and text segmentation audit of Gemini 2.5 Flash across 7 modalities (a medRxiv preprint) reports "performance, failure modes, and a fairness audit", with weaker results on ultrasound, CT and CXR and lower dermoscopy accuracy on darker ITA skin tones. Only search snippets were seen. — [medRxiv 2025.11.21.25340008](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.21.25340008.full.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Ultrasound and dermoscopy transfer:** this is covered by Medical SAM3 itself (TN3K, HC18, PH2 externally), by MedSAM-3 (BUSI, ISIC), by Lesion-SAM3 (BUSI, ISIC), by UltraSAM3 (37 ultrasound training sets plus 4 external, benchmarking Medical SAM3 directly), and by SAM and SAM2 benchmarks.
- **Point vs box vs text:** this is covered for vanilla SAM3 on BUSI, ISIC and Kvasir by Lesion-SAM3 (point, box, text, exemplar) and MedSAM-3 (text vs text+box), for pathology by Kong et al., and for 3D by Chakrabarty & Soni (click, box, mask). What has not been found is a point/box/text sweep of the fine-tuned Medical SAM3 checkpoint on held-out ultrasound and dermoscopy. That is a narrow, incremental gap, not an open research question.

### Gaps
- No full text was read for the Expert Systems "SAM 1 to SAM 3" benchmark, the medRxiv Gemini audit, or the Springer version of Lesion-SAM3.
- The exact modality list for MedSAM's 10 modalities was not re-verified this session. Prior knowledge says it includes dermoscopy and ultrasound, but treat this as unverified.

## Q4. Is a per-modality prompt-selection protocol and failure taxonomy still novel as of Nov 2026?

### Takeaway
Only marginally. Parts of both already exist: per-modality "which prompt works best" analyses (Cheng et al. 2023; Huang et al. 2024), a SAM3 evaluation protocol with prompt-type recommendations (Kong et al., ICIP 2026), quantified failure-mode categories (Chakrabarty & Soni; MedSAM-3's "Findings"; Huang et al.'s failure-factor correlations), and a survey with a hierarchical taxonomy of SAM prompt engineering (Jiang et al. 2025/2026). No single paper was found that combines a cross-modality prompt-selection decision protocol with an image-level failure taxonomy for SAM3-family medical models specifically. A well-executed, released version of that combination is defensible as a contribution, but its novelty is modest.

### Cited Findings
- Per-modality prompt choice: "We also explore what kind of prompt can lead to the best zero-shot performance with different modalities." — [Cheng et al., arXiv 2305.00035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00035)
- Failure factors: SAM's performance "correlated to different factors, including boundary complexity, intensity differences", plus sensitivity to point and box randomness. — [Huang et al., arXiv 2304.14660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14660)
- Protocol plus guidance for SAM3: "we propose a systematic evaluation protocol to explore the capability space of SAM3 in a structured manner... provides practical guidance". — [Kong et al., arXiv 2604.18225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18225)
- Named, quantified failure modes for SAM 2/3 propagation: prompt-frame over-segmentation, over-propagation after disappearance, and temporal retention decay. — [Chakrabarty & Soni, arXiv 2511.21926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21926)
- Named failure findings for SAM 3 text prompting: concept–region misalignment and poor discrimination of fine-grained terminology. — [Liu et al., arXiv 2511.19046](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.19046)
- Base-paper failure list: near-empty masks, over-segmentation collapse, false positives on thin structures, fragmentation of small targets, and background activation on dermoscopy. The authors call for "standardized prompt protocols". — [arXiv 2601.10880 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10880)
- Survey taxonomy: "a hierarchical taxonomy that organizes methods into geometric prompts, textual semantic prompts, and multimodal fusion prompts", identifying challenges such as "prompt sensitivity, cross-modal misalignment". — [Jiang et al., arXiv 2507.09562 (updated 2026-08-16)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09562)
- Text-prompt wording sensitivity ("lesion" 62.36% vs "tumor" 0.00% Dice) comes from a search snippet of Lesion-SAM3 and was not located in the PDF text. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42492-026-00234-0)

### Inferences
- **Distance between the current student project and the card:**
  1. Model mismatch: the project uses SAM ViT-B (SAM v1), while the card's base is SAM 3 / Medical SAM3, which adds text prompts, 1008 px input and a detector–tracker.
  2. Modality mismatch: the project uses only colonoscopy (Kvasir-SEG), which is in Medical SAM3's training data. The card asks for cross-modality transfer.
  3. Task mismatch: the project studies corrective unlearning of label-noise corruption, while the card asks for prompt-type transfer evaluation. No prompt-type variation, text prompts, ultrasound or dermoscopy, or prompt-selection protocol appears in the current project.
  4. No reproduction of Table 3 in the current project.
  Overall, the project addresses essentially none of Reproduce, Extend or Contribute. Unlearning is orthogonal to the card's question.
- **What an aligned project would minimally require:**
  - (i) Reproduce Table 3's external text-only rows (at least TN3K, HC18, PH2, CVC, ETIS) with the released Medical SAM3 checkpoint and the repo's 2D eval.
  - (ii) Since ultrasound and dermoscopy are already tested, pick held-out public sets not in Table 1 or Table 3 and not used by UltraSAM3's external set. Candidates are not verified against all corpora: for dermoscopy, HAM10000 segmentation masks or ISIC 2016/2017 (these overlap ISIC 2018 imagery, which risks leakage); for ultrasound, DDTI and TG3K (loaders already exist in the repo), or echocardiography such as CAMUS.
  - (iii) Run a point / box (tight GT, jittered, detector-derived) / text (canonical, synonyms) / text+box sweep on Medical SAM3, vanilla SAM3, and ideally MedSAM or BiomedParse as baselines.
  - (iv) Derive a per-modality decision protocol and an image-level failure taxonomy (for example empty mask, wrong-region grounding, over-segmentation collapse, fragmentation, boundary leakage, and thin- or small-target miss, building on the categories above), with released code.
  - (v) Cite and differentiate from Lesion-SAM3, MedSAM-3 (Liu), Kong et al., Chakrabarty & Soni, and UltraSAM3.
- If the team keeps unlearning, the only natural bridge to the card is unlearning a modality or dataset (for example, Kvasir-SEG or ISIC 2018) from Medical SAM3 and measuring cross-modality and prompt-type side effects.
